# Talk Talk — Deep Dive

## Overview

Talk Talk was an English band (1981-1991) led by singer and songwriter Mark Hollis, with Paul Webb on bass, Lee Harris on drums and, from 1984, producer, keyboard player and co-writer Tim Friese-Greene. They began as a melancholy synth-pop group on EMI, often compared to Duran Duran, and had European hits with "Today", "It's My Life" and "Such a Shame". With *The Colour of Spring* (1986) they dropped synths for piano, organ and real instruments; with *Spirit of Eden* (1988) and *Laughing Stock* (1991) they abandoned pop structure altogether, building long, quiet, dynamic pieces from months of improvised overdubs edited down in the studio. Those last two albums are credited, with Slint's work, as the beginnings of post-rock.

**Genre positioning:** Synth-pop and new wave (1982-1984), art pop (1986), then art rock and proto-post-rock (1988-1991). Hollis's guiding principle: "The silence is above everything, and I would rather hear one note than I would two."

**Commercial scale:** Bigger in continental Europe than in Britain: "Such a Shame" went to No. 1 in Switzerland and Italy, and *It's My Life* went Platinum in the Netherlands. *The Colour of Spring* reached No. 8 in the UK and No. 1 in the Netherlands. The 1990 compilation *Natural History* reached No. 3 in the UK and sold over a million copies, and the 1990 reissue of "It's My Life" was their highest UK single (No. 13). *Spirit of Eden* sold poorly at first and led to lawsuits with EMI.

**Key quote:** "Before you play two notes, learn how to play one note. And don't play one note unless you've got a reason to play it." — Mark Hollis

## Members

| Member | Role | Years |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Mark Hollis** | Vocals, guitar, piano, organ, melodica; main songwriter | 1981-1991 (died 2019) |
| **Lee Harris** | Drums | 1981-1991 |
| **Paul Webb** | Bass | 1981-1988 |
| **Simon Brenner** | Keyboards | 1981-1983 |
| **Tim Friese-Greene** | Producer, keyboards (piano, organ, harmonium), co-writer | 1984-1991; never an official member |

**Notes:**
- Hollis had led the punk band The Reaction (1977-1979); their "Talk Talk Talk Talk" became the first Talk Talk single
- Friese-Greene produced four albums and, from *The Colour of Spring*, co-wrote every song with Hollis, but did not tour or appear in publicity
- Webb left in late 1988 and does not play on *Laughing Stock*
- After the split, Webb and Harris formed .O.rang (1992-1996); Webb records as Rustin Man (*Out of Season* with Beth Gibbons, 2002). Hollis released one solo album (1998), then retired from music. He died in February 2019, aged 64

**Regular session players:** Mark Feltham (harmonica), Henry Lowther (trumpet, flugelhorn), Martin Ditcham (percussion), Danny Thompson (double bass), Robbie McIntosh (guitars); engineer Phill Brown on the last two albums.

## Sonic Evolution Summary

| Era | Releases | Sound |
|-----|----------|-------|
| 1982 | *The Party's Over* | Synth-pop and new wave: synths, drum machine-tight beats, Hollis's yearning voice; "One sad mood permeates the whole LP" (Neil Tennant, Smash Hits) |
| 1984 | *It's My Life* | "Mournful synth-pop" with more space and guest players (trumpet, piano, percussion) |
| 1986 | *The Colour of Spring* | Art pop on real instruments: piano, Hammond organ, Mellotron, harmonica, double bass, choir; long songs. Hollis: synths were "simply an economic measure… if they didn't exist, I'd be delighted" |
| 1988 | *Spirit of Eden* | Six long, quiet-loud pieces assembled from improvised overdubs: organ, harmonium, harmonica, trumpet, double bass, woodwinds, a cathedral choir; silence as an instrument |
| 1991 | *Laughing Stock* | Sparser and rawer still: room-miked drums, violas and cellos, contrabass clarinet, guitar feedback; long tracks; "arranged spontaneity" |

**Tempo range:** Spotify-derived readings run from about 77 BPM ("April 5th") to the mid-150s for early singles, but tempo matters less on the late records, where pieces drift and change density rather than hold a groove.

## Studio Albums

### The Party's Over (1982)

- **Release date**: 12 July 1982
- **Label**: EMI
- **Producer**: Colin Thurston (chosen for his work on Bowie's "Heroes")
- **Length**: 36:28
- **Chart**: UK 21 (OCC; Wikipedia gives 23), New Zealand 8; BPI Silver

Trouser Press (1983) heard Duran Duran's "surface sheen" without their "panache or underlying passion"; Neil Tennant (Smash Hits) noted that "one sad mood permeates the whole LP".

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Today"** (~153 BPM, 3:28) — First UK Top 20 hit
- **"Talk Talk"** (G, ~135 BPM, 3:20) — Reworking of the Reaction song; UK 23 when re-recorded

- **Release date**: 13 February 1984
- **Label**: EMI / EMI America
- **Producer**: Tim Friese-Greene

- **Length**: 43:13

### The Colour of Spring (1986)

- **Release date**: 17 February 1986
- **Label**: EMI
- **Producer**: Tim Friese-Greene
- **Recording**: Battery Studios and Videosonics, London, 1985
- **Guests**: Steve Winwood (organ), Mark Feltham (harmonica), Danny Thompson (acoustic bass), Robbie McIntosh and David Rhodes (guitars), David Roach (soprano saxophone), Gaynor Sadler (harp), a children's choir and the Ambrosian Singers
- **Length**: 45:41
- **Chart**: UK 8, Netherlands 1, Switzerland 3, US 58; Gold in the UK, Netherlands and Canada

"The first indication of his greater ambitions" (The Quietus). Friese-Greene: "MIDI is a four-letter word." The palette moved to acoustic piano, Hammond organ, Mellotron, melodica and Variophon.

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Life's What You Make It"** (D♭, ~92 BPM, 4:29) — Built on a drum pattern "loosely inspired by Kate Bush's 'Running Up That Hill'" (Friese-Greene); a hammering piano figure; UK 16
- **"Living in Another World"** (A, ~111 BPM, 6:58) — Organ, harmonica and a long build
- **"Happiness Is Easy"** (F, ~107 BPM, 6:30) — Opens the album with a children's choir and double bass
- **"April 5th"** (A♭, ~77 BPM, 5:51) — Slow and still; a sign of what came next

**Tracklist:** Happiness Is Easy 6:30 · I Don't Believe in You 5:02 · Life's What You Make It 4:29 · April 5th 5:51 · Living in Another World 6:58 · Give It Up 5:17 · Chameleon Day 3:20 · Time It's Time 8:14

### Spirit of Eden (1988)

- **Release date**: 12 September 1988
- **Label**: Parlophone (EMI)
- **Producer**: Tim Friese-Greene; engineered by Phill Brown
- **Studio**: Wessex Studios, London, May 1987 – March 1988
- **Personnel**: Hollis (vocals, guitar, organ, melodica, piano, Variophon), Friese-Greene (harmonium, piano, organ, guitar), Lee Harris, Paul Webb, Martin Ditcham (percussion), Robbie McIntosh (dobro, 12-string guitar), Mark Feltham (harmonica), Danny Thompson (double bass), Henry Lowther (trumpet), Nigel Kennedy (violin), bassoon, oboe, clarinet and cor anglais, the Choir of Chelmsford Cathedral
- **Length**: About 41 minutes
- **Chart**: UK 19, Germany 16, Switzerland 12; BPI Silver

Made from long improvised sessions, often in near-darkness lit by oil projectors and a strobe, and then edited; lyrics and vocals came last. Brown: "It takes a strong discipline to erase 80% of the music you record", and "Twelve hours a day in the dark listening to the same six songs for eight months became pretty intense." Q (1988) called it "a brave record that is not afraid to follow its own muse and damn the consequences" and "the kind of record that encourages marketing men to commit suicide". Its standing grew later: Uncut 10/10 (2012), Pitchfork 10/10 (2019).

**Sonic Palette:**
- Long silences and very quiet passages, broken by sudden loud entries
- Organ and harmonium drones, harmonica, trumpet, double bass, woodwinds, choir
- Guitar used for texture and sudden noise
- Hollis's voice used sparingly, high and fragile
- Six pieces of five to nine minutes with no conventional verse-chorus form

**Key Tracks:**
- **"The Rainbow"** (E, 9:05) — Opens from near-silence; harmonica and guitar; the longest piece. songbpm gives both 85 and 168 BPM
- **"I Believe in You"** (F♯, ~91 BPM, 6:11) — The single; Hollis called its video "a massive mistake"
- **"Inheritance"** (G, ~116 BPM, 5:16) — The album's steadiest pulse
- **"Wealth"** (6:35) — The closer: organ and a "naked breathed voice" (Drowned in Sound)

**Tracklist:** The Rainbow 9:05 · Eden 6:37 · Desire 6:57 · Inheritance 5:16 · I Believe in You 6:11 · Wealth 6:35

### Laughing Stock (1991)

- **Release date**: 16 September 1991
- **Label**: Verve / Polydor
- **Producer**: Tim Friese-Greene; engineered by Phill Brown
- **Studio**: Wessex Studios, 1990-1991
- **Personnel**: Hollis, Harris, Friese-Greene (piano, organ, harmonium), Mark Feltham (harmonica), Martin Ditcham (percussion), Henry Lowther (trumpet, flugelhorn), Dave White (contrabass clarinet), seven violas, two cellos, two acoustic bassists; about 50 musicians were recorded and 18 kept
- **Length**: 43:29
- **Chart**: UK 26

The final album. Players improvised over sections "without hearing the full track" (manager Keith Aspden). Hollis: "It's arranged spontaneity – that's exactly what it is." Brown: it was "recorded by chance, accident, and hours of trying every possible overdub idea". Melody Maker (1991): "three masterpieces in a row"; NME: "unutterably pretentious". Stylus (2007) named it the greatest post-rock album.

**Sonic Palette:**
- Drums recorded only by a distant valve microphone in the room (about 28-30 feet away) plus a kick-drum mic, giving a large, delayed, natural sound
- Violas, cellos, contrabass clarinet, trumpet, organ, harmonica
- Feedback and reversed guitar, sudden noise
- Long tracks (up to nine and a half minutes) that move from near-silence to bursts and back

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Myrrhman"** (5:33) — Sparse, slow opener
- **"Ascension Day"** (6:00) — "Like a small jazz combo being elbowed aside by a noise rock band" (Pitchfork); ends abruptly
- **"After the Flood"** (B♭, ~89 BPM, 9:39) — Organ, piano and feedback guitar over the distant drums; Brown's "best engineering… in the past forty years"
- **"New Grass"** (A, ~100 BPM, 9:40) — Steady, gentle groove; the most serene piece

**Tracklist:** Myrrhman 5:33 · Ascension Day 6:00 · After the Flood 9:39 · Taphead 7:39 · New Grass 9:40 · Runeii 4:58

## Musical Analysis

### Silence and Restraint

Silence is the defining element of the late records. Hollis: "I think silence is an extremely important thing", and "ninety percent of what you play will be rubbish". Critic Simon Harper wrote that the band used silence "almost as an instrument in its own right". Pieces start from near-nothing, hold single notes and leave long gaps, so a single loud entry (a guitar burst, a full band chord) lands with great force.

### Improvisation and Editing

On *Spirit of Eden* and *Laughing Stock* the songs were not played through by a band. Hollis and Friese-Greene built basic tracks; musicians were brought in, often one at a time and sometimes in the dark, to improvise over sections they heard in their headphones; the best moments were then kept and the rest erased, over many months. Friese-Greene on Hollis: "He was in a darkened room for a year listening to the same six tracks." It is "arranged spontaneity": composed by selection.

### Instruments over Synthesizers

From 1986 the band moved away from synths ("MIDI is a four-letter word") to acoustic piano, Hammond organ, harmonium, Mellotron, melodica and Variophon, plus harmonica (Mark Feltham), trumpet and flugelhorn (Henry Lowther), double bass (Danny Thompson), strings, woodwinds and choirs.

### Recording the Room

Phill Brown's late-period drum sound comes from one valve microphone placed far from the kit plus a kick-drum microphone; close microphones fed only the players' headphones and were never recorded. The result is spacious and slightly delayed, as if heard from across a hall.

### Hollis's Voice

High, fragile and often half-sung, sometimes barely above a breath; used "as an instrument as part of the soundscape". The lyrics on the late albums are short, spiritual and imagistic.

### Influences Hollis Named

Miles Davis and Gil Evans (*Sketches of Spain*, *Porgy and Bess*: "space, tight arrangement and technique but it also has movement within it"), John Coltrane, Bartók's string quartets ("I'd never imagined something so beautiful existed"), Debussy, Satie, Milhaud, Traffic, Van Morrison, Can's *Tago Mago*, Bob Dylan's *New Morning*. Webb adds Van Morrison's *Astral Weeks* and King Crimson's *In the Court of the Crimson King*. Early on Hollis called the band "closer to a jazz quartet than a rock band", but he resisted the jazz label for *Laughing Stock*.

### Genre Classification

- **Synth-pop / new wave**: *The Party's Over*, *It's My Life*
- **Art pop**: *The Colour of Spring*
- **Art rock / post-rock**: *Spirit of Eden*, *Laughing Stock*, credited with Slint as post-rock's beginnings

## Reception and Influence

| Album | Selected verdicts |
|-------|-------------------|
| *The Party's Over* | Trouser Press: "slick and professional but lifeless" |
| *It's My Life* | "Mournful synth-pop" (The Quietus); big in Europe |
| *The Colour of Spring* | UK 8; "the first indication of his greater ambitions" |
| *Spirit of Eden* | Q: "a brave record"; later Uncut 10/10, Mojo 5/5, Pitchfork 10/10 |
| *Laughing Stock* | Melody Maker: "three masterpieces in a row"; NME: "unutterably pretentious"; Stylus: greatest post-rock album |

**Influence:** Paste credited *Spirit of Eden* with paving the way for Sigur Rós, Mogwai, Godspeed You! Black Emperor, Low and later Radiohead. Bark Psychosis cite both late albums and worked with Phill Brown; Elbow's Guy Garvey calls them his favourites; Doves, Bon Iver, M83, Steven Wilson and others are cited as influenced.
