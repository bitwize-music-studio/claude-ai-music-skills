# Cherry Poppin' Daddies — Deep Dive

## Overview

Cherry Poppin' Daddies are a band from Eugene, Oregon, formed in 1989 by singer, songwriter and guitarist Steve Perry (not the Journey singer) and bassist Dan Schmid. A punk band with a horn section from the start, they have recorded swing, jump blues, ska, ska-punk, funk, rockabilly, glam, Latin and psychedelic records, usually keeping each style separate rather than blending them. Their 1997 compilation of swing songs, *Zoot Suit Riot: The Swingin' Hits of the Cherry Poppin' Daddies*, reached No. 17 on the Billboard 200 and went double Platinum, and its title song became one of the signature records of the late-1990s swing revival. They have kept recording since, with fourteen studio albums by 2026.

**Genre positioning:** Neo-swing, ska, ska-punk, punk; critics coined "punk swing" and "big band punk rock". Perry calls them "a rock band with horns" and "a rocking dance band with horns": "The idea was to merge punk rock sensibilities with this swing thing… We wanted to be a hybrid." They are credited with helping to start the swing revival and paving the way for Big Bad Voodoo Daddy and the Brian Setzer Orchestra; *Zoot Suit Riot* was the first swing-revival album to reach the Billboard Top 40.

**Commercial scale:** *Zoot Suit Riot* (Billboard 200 No. 17, 53 weeks; Heatseekers No. 1) is certified double Platinum in the US and Gold in Canada and New Zealand. The single "Zoot Suit Riot" reached No. 41 on the Hot 100 Airplay chart and No. 15 on Modern Rock (1998); its video was among MTV's most-requested of 1998.

**Key quote:** "We wanted to be The Rolling Stones of swing, not The Beatles of swing." — Steve Perry, 2016

**The name:** Jive slang the members heard on a vintage "race" record. Perry: "we thought it was hilarious and perfect." It provoked protests in Eugene — picketing, torn-down posters, boycotts, a bomb threat — and venues stopped booking them, so they played locally as "The Daddies". Perry later called it "probably the most heinous name in the history of rock".

The band has had more than forty members; there were at least fifteen line-up changes in 1996-1997 alone. Perry and Heitman are the only constants.

**Steve Perry:** Born 1963 in Syracuse, New York; came to Eugene in 1981 for track and field and chemistry at the University of Oregon, dropped out, and later earned a degree in molecular biology (2004); he works in a biology lab. Before the Daddies he led the punk trio The Jazz Greats, Saint Huck and Mr. Wiggles (with a horn section led by Brooks Brown). He also formed White Hot Odyssey (2002-2005) with Jason Moss.

## Albums

### Ferociously Stoned (1990)

- **Release date**: 20 November 1990 (reissued 1994)
- **Label**: Sub Par; later Space Age Bachelor Pad
- **Producers**: The band and Bill Barnett; Gung Ho Studios, Eugene

Punk and funk with jazz and swing, psychedelic pop, disco and James Brown-style R&B. It nearly set a record for advance sales in Eugene and spent over a year on *The Rocket*'s Northwest Top 20.

**Key Tracks:** "Drunk Daddy" (A minor, ~110 BPM, 5:11) · "Master and Slave" (4:07) · "Shake Your Lovemaker" (5:51); the reissue adds "Dr. Bones" (C major, ~107 BPM, 3:32) and "Cherry Poppin' Daddy Strut"

### Rapid City Muscle Car (1994)

- **Release date**: 12 December 1994
- **Label**: Space Age Bachelor Pad
- **Length**: 53:53
- **Reception**: AllMusic 4/5; Perry's favourite of their albums

**Key Tracks:** "Sockable Face Club" (~110 BPM, 3:46) · "The Ding-Dong Daddy of the D-Car Line" (3:34) · "Mister White Keys" (3:08) · "Come Back to Me" (2:40) · "The Impossible Dream" (4:35)

### Kids on the Street (1996)

- **Release date**: 6 February 1996
- **Label**: Space Age Bachelor Pad / Caroline
- **Producer**: Steve Perry
- **Length**: 52:26
- **Sales**: About 25,000; on *Rolling Stone*'s Alternative chart

Guitar-driven ska and punk; Perry moved to rhythm guitar.

**Key Tracks:** "Here Comes the Snake" (B minor, ~106 BPM, 3:22) · "Kids on the Street" (3:17) · "Irish Whiskey" (3:42) · "Cosa Nostra" (3:50)

### Zoot Suit Riot: The Swingin' Hits of the Cherry Poppin' Daddies (1997)

- **Release date**: 18 March 1997 (own label); 1 July 1997 (Mojo)
- **Producers**: Steve Perry and the band; Gung Ho Studios
- **Line-up**: Perry, Dana Heitman (trumpet, trombone), Ian Early (alto, tenor), Sean Flannery (tenor), Jason Moss (guitar), Darren Cassidy (bass), Tim Donahue (drums)
- **Length**: 51:28
- **Chart**: Billboard 200 No. 17 (53 weeks), Heatseekers No. 1, New Zealand No. 5; RIAA double Platinum
- **Reception**: The LA Times: "the most effective music for the dance fad of the moment"; All About Jazz: "The horn parts, meaty and athletic, are mixed to the fore"; AllMusic: "smirking hipster joke"; Christgau: "Dud"

A compilation of the band's swing songs from earlier albums plus new recordings, first sold at shows. Perry: "a blessing as well as a curse".

**Tracklist:** Zoot Suit Riot 3:53 · The Ding-Dong Daddy of the D-Car Line 3:31 · When I Change Your Mind 2:29 · Here Comes the Snake 3:18 · Mr. White Keys 3:07 · Come Back to Me 2:39 · Brown Derby Jump 2:58 · Dr. Bones 3:34 · Pink Elephant 3:40 · Master and Slave 4:04 · Drunk Daddy 5:10 · No Mercy for Swine 4:02 · Cherry Poppin' Daddy Strut 3:05 · Shake Your Lovemaker 5:50

### Soul Caddy (2000)

- **Release date**: 3 October 2000
- **Label**: Mojo
- **Producers**: Steve Perry, with Tony Visconti and Jack Joseph Puig; mastered by Bob Ludwig
- **Length**: 43:33
- **Reception**: Mostly negative (Entertainment Weekly C−: "insufferable enthusiasm"); AllMusic: "flat-out fun and there's no way around that"

A loose concept album about fame and alienation, in glam, soul, psychedelic pop, rockabilly and punk. It did not chart, the tour ended early and the band went on hiatus.

**Key Tracks:** "Diamond Light Boogie" (F♯ major, ~187 BPM, 3:42) · "Soul Cadillac" (A major, ~115 BPM, 3:29) · "So Long Toots" (F, ~188 BPM, 2:30)

### Susquehanna (2008)

- **Release date**: 2008 (digital February, CD June)
- **Label**: Space Age Bachelor Pad (reissued by Rock Ridge)
- **Producer**: Steve Perry

Latin and Caribbean styles — flamenco, soca, bossa nova and reggae — on "various relationships in decay". PopMatters: Perry's "pipes sound dry and bland amidst the complex surroundings" (4/10); other critics called his voice a "secret weapon".

**Key Tracks:** "Hammerblow" (B minor, ~173 BPM, 3:00) · "Bust Out" (4:08) · "Arráncate" (4:07)

### White Teeth, Black Thoughts (2013)

- **Release date**: 16 July 2013
- **Producer**: Steve Perry

A return to swing, with an Americana bonus disc and Great Recession themes; includes a Louis Jordan cover ("Doug the Jitterbug"). PopMatters: "a cocktail of swing, jazz, punk, ska, and everything in between"; AllMusic: "an album of exuberant, urbane dance music".

**Key Tracks:** "I Love American Music" (3:47) · "The Babooch" (3:57) · "Brown Flight Jacket" (4:07)

### Later Albums

- ***Please Return the Evening*** (2014): Rat Pack standards ("The Best Is Yet to Come", "Come Fly With Me", "Luck Be a Lady", "That's Life")

## "Zoot Suit Riot"

- **Credits**: Written and produced by Steve Perry; 3:53
- **Subject**: Loosely based on the 1943 Los Angeles Zoot Suit Riots, when servicemen attacked young Mexican-Americans; Perry calls it "a Pachuco rallying cry that could double as a dance anthem" and "an expression of a proud marginalism", and cites Lalo Guerrero as an influence. Wikipedia notes it has "no overt political or social commentary"
- **Recording**: Cut in one take to save money; Perry's spoken aside "I think I'm about ready to sing it" was left on the record
- **Sound**: "1940s jump blues": fast swing (about 168-184 BPM by different measures) in a minor key (A minor in one published edition; G♯ minor by Spotify's analysis), with alto, tenor and baritone saxophones, trumpet and trombone over a four-piece rhythm section
- **Videos**: The first was panned; the second, a surreal, occult video by Gregory Dark inspired by Buñuel, was among MTV's most-requested of 1998 and earned a VMA nomination
- **Charts**: Hot 100 Airplay No. 41, Modern Rock No. 15, Adult Top 40 No. 16, Canada No. 13, UK No. 81
- **Afterlife**: Parodied by "Weird Al" Yankovic ("Grapefruit Diet", 1999); covered by Ray Anthony; used in *Urban Legend*

## Musical Analysis

### A Rock Band with Horns

The core is a rock rhythm section (guitar, bass, drums, sometimes keys) plus a horn section of trumpet and two or three saxophones, later with trombone. In the swing songs the horns play riffs, stabs and shout choruses in the manner of 1940s jump blues; in the ska songs they play offbeat lines; in the punk songs they double the guitars.

### Genre by Album

Rather than fusing styles, the band usually plays each one straight and moves between them, song to song or album to album. Perry: "if we had stuck to one style, we'd have been easier to market", and "Sometimes working in an opposite genre or an unexpected genre can add impact and power."

### Influences

Fishbone, Oingo Boingo, the Specials and Roxy Music; big-band leaders Fletcher Henderson, Jimmie Lunceford and Duke Ellington; the Wipers, Little Richard and the Kinks; Lalo Guerrero for "Zoot Suit Riot".

### Lyrics

Narrative songs with unreliable narrators, in the line of Randy Newman, Ray Davies and Jarvis Cocker; recurring subjects are sex, death, working-class life, alcoholism and alienation, with dark humour and satire. Most albums are concept albums.

### On the Swing Revival

Perry resisted the "retro" label: "it's not our mission to be a swing band". He thought the revival should have pushed for "making it modern" rather than "scoobie dooing around like a freckle-faced Opie", and feared it could end "just like disco".

### Genre Classification

- **Neo-swing / jump blues**: *Zoot Suit Riot*, *White Teeth, Black Thoughts*, *At the Pink Rat*
- **Ska / ska-punk**: *Kids on the Street*, *Skaboy JFK*, *Bigger Life*, *Roma! Roma!*
- **Punk and funk with horns**: *Ferociously Stoned*
- **Eclectic rock**: *Rapid City Muscle Car*, *Soul Caddy*, *Susquehanna*
