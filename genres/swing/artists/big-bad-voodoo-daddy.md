# Big Bad Voodoo Daddy — Deep Dive

## Overview

Big Bad Voodoo Daddy is a seven-piece swing band from Ventura, California, founded in 1989 by singer and guitarist Scotty Morris, a former hardcore-punk bassist, with drummer Kurt Sodergren. Their Wednesday-night residency at the Derby in Los Angeles put them in the 1996 film *Swingers*, and their 1998 Capitol album *Americana Deluxe* (also issued as *Big Bad Voodoo Daddy*) went Platinum at the height of the swing revival. They played "Go Daddy-O" at the Super Bowl XXXIII halftime show in 1999. Where some revival bands mixed swing with punk or ska, they stayed close to traditional jump blues and swing — Cab Calloway, Louis Armstrong, Louis Jordan and Louis Prima — and outlasted the revival, later making tribute albums to those heroes and touring theatres, orchestras and festivals with a line-up largely unchanged since the late 1990s.

**Genre positioning:** Neo-swing, jump blues, jazz; "jazz, swing, Dixieland, and big-band music" from a seven-piece group (band bio). Wikipedia's swing-revival article places them with Royal Crown Revue as bands that "often stayed close to traditional jump blues and rockabilly"; a critic calls them "one of the most dynamic little big bands".

**Commercial scale:** *Americana Deluxe* reached No. 47 on the Billboard 200 and was certified Platinum in 1999; with *This Beautiful Life* (No. 93) it sold over two million copies. Their only charting single, "You & Me & the Bottle Makes 3 Tonight (Baby)", reached No. 31 on Alternative Songs. Later albums led the jazz charts (*Rattle Them Bones* No. 2, *Louie Louie Louie* No. 7). About 3,000 shows, including the Hollywood Bowl and Lincoln Center.

**Key quote:** "I was a punk rock kid at heart, still am." — Scotty Morris, 2026

**The name:** Blues guitarist Albert Collins signed a poster for Morris "To Scotty, the big bad voodoo daddy".

| **Scotty Morris** | Lead vocals, guitar; songwriter and producer |
| **Kurt Sodergren** | Drums, percussion (co-founder) |

| **Andy Rowley** | Baritone saxophone (tenor on early records), vocals |
| **Glen "The Kid" Marhevka** | Trumpet, cornet |
| **Karl Hunter** | Tenor, alto and soprano saxophone, clarinet |
| **Joshua Levy** | Piano; arranger and co-producer since 2009 |

Touring players: Alex "Crazy Legs" Henderson (trombone), Mitch Cooper (lead trumpet). Former: Ralph Votrian (trumpet, 1994), Jeff Harris (trombone, *Americana Deluxe*). Later press calls all seven "original members", though Hunter, Marhevka and Levy are not on the 1994 album; the line-up "remains intact" as of 2026.

**Scotty Morris:** Born in Ventura; first bassist of the Oxnard hardcore band False Confession; later a studio musician. At eight or nine he heard "Minnie the Moocher" in a Betty Boop cartoon. He left punk because "the punk rock scene got really violent in Los Angeles and it wasn't fun anymore."

## Musical Analysis

### A Little Big Band

Seven players: trumpet, two saxophonists doubling clarinet and baritone, piano, double bass, drums and Morris's guitar, with trombone and a second trumpet added live — five horns playing big-band charts with careful arranging. The rhythm section is acoustic (double bass, swing drums, piano), and the horns play riffs, stabs, harmonised section lines and shout choruses. Their clinics cite Fletcher Henderson and Count Basie, and trumpeter Glen Marhevka teaches Basie-style soft dynamics.

### The Repertoire Behind Them

Cab Calloway (the hi-de-ho call-and-response of "Minnie the Moocher"), Louis Armstrong ("When I heard Armstrong's music, that was it for me"), Louis Jordan's jump blues, Louis Prima's party swing, and New Orleans jazz. Morris's originals ("Go Daddy-O", "You & Me & the Bottle Makes 3 Tonight", "Mr. Pinstripe Suit") write new songs in that idiom: "I try hard to write songs that transcend time."

### Morris's Voice and the Punk Spirit

A swing-shouter and crooner voice ("a great swing voice", musiccritic.com). The Daily Bruin (1998) heard a "quasi-punk spirit" without a "sneering attitude"; punk kids danced at their all-ages shows. Musically, though, they kept to traditional swing rather than punk or ska hybrids.

### How They Differ from Other Revival Bands

- **Cherry Poppin' Daddies**: Punk and ska with horns, genre by genre
- **Brian Setzer Orchestra**: Rockabilly guitar fronting a full big band
- **Big Bad Voodoo Daddy**: A traditional jump-blues and swing combo with original songs

### The Super Bowl and After

The NFL first approached Brian Setzer. Most of the halftime performance was pre-recorded, with Morris playing the guitar riffs live; he later said the staging turned swing into "this clowny, zoot suit feather-in-your-hat kind of thing" and "I don't think it could happen for a band like us again." Rob Sheffield wrote that the show "did for the '90s what Altamont did for the '60s"; alternative radio dropped the band. Their response was to keep touring and move to theatres, orchestras and the jazz charts.

### Genre Classification

- **Neo-swing / swing revival**: The primary label
- **Jump blues**: Louis Jordan and Cab Calloway lineage
- **New Orleans jazz / Dixieland**: *Save My Soul*, the Christmas albums, *Louie Louie Louie*
