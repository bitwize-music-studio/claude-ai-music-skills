# Justice — Deep Dive

## Overview

Justice are the Parisian duo Gaspard Augé and Xavier de Rosnay, two former graphic designers who became the flagship act of Ed Banger Records. Their 2007 debut *†* (*Cross*) took the filtered-disco tradition of French house and pushed it into distorted, compressed, rock-loud electro, and set off a wave of imitators. Each album since has changed direction: progressive rock on *Audio, Video, Disco.* (2011), cold programmed disco with a choir and strings on *Woman* (2016), and polished synth-disco on *Hyperdrama* (2024). In October 2026 Rockstar confirmed them as the composers of the original score for *Grand Theft Auto VI*.

**Genre positioning:** French house, electro house, nu-disco; in French press, "French Touch 2.0". Resident Advisor credits them with "a new wave of electro house that weaved in elements of rock and disco", and *Cross* with helping "establish Ed Banger as the flagbearer for French electro". Ed Banger founder Pedro Winter (Busy P) has his own label for it: "I prefer 'heavy metal disco.'"

**Commercial scale:** *Cross* reached No. 1 on the UK and US dance album charts and was certified Gold in the UK; "D.A.N.C.E." is certified Gold in the US. Grammys: Best Remixed Recording for MGMT's "Electric Feel" (2009), Best Dance/Electronic Album for *Woman Worldwide* (2019) and Best Dance/Electronic Recording for "Neverender" with Tame Impala (2025). *Hyperdrama* reached No. 2 in France.

**Key quote:** *Audio, Video, Disco.* is "a progressive rock record played by guys who don't know how to play." — the band, 2011

## Members

| Member | Role | Years |
|--------|------|-------|
| **Gaspard Augé** | Production, composition, keyboards | 2003-present |
| **Xavier de Rosnay** | Production, composition, keyboards | 2003-present |

Both write and produce everything; they describe themselves as "each other's Rick Rubin" (de Rosnay, 2024). They met at a kitchen party thrown by mutual friends, when both were graphic designers in their early twenties; de Rosnay: "Listening to Buggles and Camel — that's where we met." Augé released a solo album, *Escapades*, in 2021.

**Frequent collaborators:** So Me (Bertrand de Langeron), Ed Banger's art director, who designs their covers and videos; vocalists Morgan Phalen, Uffie, Ali Love and Vincent Vendetta; Tame Impala, Thundercat, Miguel and Connan Mockasin on *Hyperdrama*; drummer Vincent Taeger and synth player and mixer Vincent Taurelle on *Hyperdrama*.

### Visual Identity

- **The cross**: The "T" in JUSTICE could be replaced by a cross, following "heavy-metal and rock style logo design" (de Rosnay). Titling an album with a symbol came from Pink Floyd's *Dark Side of the Moon*
- **Covers**: So Me's *†* cover is a homage to T. Rex's *Electric Warrior*
- **The live show**: A glowing cross flanked by two walls of Marshall amplifier cabinets (dummy fronts), with the music run from Ableton Live

## Studio Albums

### † (Cross) (2007)

- **Release date**: 11 June 2007
- **Label**: Ed Banger / Because (Downtown/Vice in the US)
- **Producers**: Gaspard Augé, Xavier de Rosnay
- **Recording**: Paris, 2005-2006
- **Length**: 48:13
- **Chart**: UK Dance 1, US Dance 1, France 11, UK 49; BPI Gold
- **Reception**: Metacritic 81; in *1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die*

The band called it an "opera-disco" album. It is built from "microsamples" taken from about 400 records: "We do sample really small bits of things that nobody can recognize." PopMatters (2007) heard "a modern Big Beat aesthetic" applied "to an army of '80s keyboards". De Rosnay suggested the scene's "nu-rave" tag should be "new big beat".

**Sonic Palette:**
- Chopped, unrecognisable disco and funk samples; string chords played on a keyboard and replaced with string samples
- Distorted, heavily compressed synth bass and leads
- Big-room drums and claps; rock dynamics
- Choir and church-organ grandeur ("Genesis", "Let There Be Light")
- Guest vocals: Uffie ("Tthhee Ppaarrttyy"), Mehdi Pinson ("DVNO"); a children's choir on "D.A.N.C.E."

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Genesis"** (A major, ~117 BPM, 3:54) — The opener: a fanfare of organ, brass-like synths and drums
- **"D.A.N.C.E."** (4:02) — The breakthrough single: a children's choir (the Foundation for Young Musicians, recorded in London) over a disco groove; the chorus interpolates Britney Spears's "Me Against the Music". Grammy-nominated; US Gold
- **"Phantom"** (D major, ~117 BPM, 4:22) — Distorted, stuttering riff built from a microsample
- **"DVNO"** (G♯ major, ~120 BPM, 3:56) — Falsetto pop-funk with a spoken bridge
- **"Stress"** (B♭ minor, ~127 BPM, 4:58) — Strings and distortion mixed to "almost give you a headache" (de Rosnay); "the most claustrophobic club pounder you've ever encountered" (The Observer)
- **"Waters of Nazareth"** (D major, ~124 BPM, 4:25) — The 2005 single: grinding, overdriven electro

### Audio, Video, Disco. (2011)

- **Release date**: 24 October 2011
- **Label**: Ed Banger / Because (Elektra in the US)
- **Recording**: February 2010 – June 2011
- **Length**: 46:25
- **Chart**: France 5, US 37, UK 35
- **Reception**: Metacritic 69; Pitchfork 5.3, A.V. Club A−

"A synth-heavy prog record just as concerned with guitar riffage" (A.V. Club); "arena rock techno" (NOW Toronto). - Arena-rock riffs and drums; seventies prog chord moves
- Guest vocals: Ali Love ("Civilization"), Vincent Vendetta ("Ohio"), Morgan Phalen ("On'n'On", "New Lands")

- **"Audio, Video, Disco"** (4:52) — Title track; anthem-scale build

### Woman (2016)

- **Release date**: 18 November 2016
- **Label**: Ed Banger / Because
- **Length**: 54:10
- **Chart**: US Dance 1, France 17, UK 47

### Hyperdrama (2024)

- **Release date**: 26 April 2024
- **Label**: Ed Banger / Because
- **Producers**: Justice
- **Length**: 49:12
- **Chart**: France 2, UK Dance 1, US Dance 1
- **Reception**: Metacritic 71; NME five stars ("flashy, over the top and keen to make a spectacle"); Pitchfork: "Justice have never sounded more polished"; The Observer: "too polished and bright"
- **Awards**: "Neverender" won the 2025 Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Recording; certified Gold in France and the US

Most of its sounds come from one synthesizer: "*Hyperdrama* was pretty much made with the PPG, and the VST plugin called the WaveGenerator" (de Rosnay). Augé: "It's something we've always liked to do, to blur the lines so people don't know how it's made." Of "One Night/All Night": "This song oscillates between pure electronic music and pure disco."

**Sonic Palette:**
- PPG Wave 2.2 wavetable synth sounds throughout
- Live drums (Vincent Taeger) beside programmed ones
- Guest vocalists: Tame Impala, Thundercat, Miguel, Connan Mockasin, Rimon, The Flints
- French house, synthwave and disco blended; short interludes

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Neverender"** (with Tame Impala) (F♯ minor, ~117 BPM, 4:26) — Dreamy psych-pop vocal over French house; Grammy winner

- **"One Night/All Night"** (with Tame Impala) (C minor, ~116 BPM, 4:36) — Between "pure electronic music and pure disco"

## Musical Analysis

### Sampling and Programming

On *Cross*, the duo composed on a Roland Juno-106 and then replaced most parts with samples on a single computer, working in Cubase SX and GarageBand with LinPlug Delta plug-ins and Genelec 8030 monitors (Future Music, 2007). "The strings, for example, are chords we played and then replaced the MIDI with individual string samples." The samples are deliberately too small to recognise. This is the root of their sound: disco and funk feel, rebuilt from fragments and pushed hard.

### Distortion and Compression

The *Cross*-era sound is loud and saturated: distorted bass and leads, pumping compression, and mixes designed to overwhelm ("Stress" was mixed to "almost give you a headache"). De Rosnay on compressors: "Why limit yourself to one set of compressors? It changes every day and for every track." Later records keep the punch but pull back the distortion.

### Rock Forms on Electronic Instruments

From the start they borrowed rock's grammar: the cross logo, Marshall stacks, riffs, guitar-solo-like synth leads. *Audio, Video, Disco.* goes furthest, with guitars, Solina, Mellotron and Rhodes and the structures of seventies prog and arena rock.

### Disco and Funk

Disco is the constant underneath: the four-on-the-floor or funk groove, falsetto vocals, strings and finger snaps. *Woman* and *Hyperdrama* bring it to the front, combining programming with a real choir, real strings (*Woman*) and live drums (*Hyperdrama*).

### Vocals

They do not sing lead; guests do. The recurring voices are high and soulful (Morgan Phalen's falsetto, Romuald Lauverjon), with choirs (children's on "D.A.N.C.E.", the London Contemporary Orchestra on *Woman*), occasional rap (Uffie) and psych-pop (Tame Impala). The vocals are often treated as one more layer in the arrangement.

### Genre Classification

- **French house / French Touch 2.0**: Their lineage and their label scene
- **Electro house**: The *Cross* era
- **Nu-disco / disco-funk**: *Woman*, much of *Hyperdrama*
- **Progressive / arena rock**: *Audio, Video, Disco.*

## Reception and Influence

| Album | Metacritic | Selected verdicts |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| *†* | 81 | RA: "acerbic Moog synths and distortion pedals"; *1001 Albums* |
| *Audio, Video, Disco.* | 69 | A.V. Club A−; Pitchfork 5.3 |
| *Woman* | 69 | Mixed to positive |
| *Hyperdrama* | 71 | NME 5/5; Pitchfork: "never sounded more polished" |

RA credits *Cross* with establishing Ed Banger as French electro's flagbearer, and Dazed (2016) notes that "their ultra-distorted sound was often imitated" during the blog-house era. No direct first-person citation from another artist was found; Apple Music's editorial "Inspired by Justice" playlist groups them with Skrillex, Boys Noize, Gesaffelstein and Digitalism.
