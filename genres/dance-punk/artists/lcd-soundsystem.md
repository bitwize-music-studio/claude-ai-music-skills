# LCD Soundsystem — Deep Dive

## Overview

LCD Soundsystem is James Murphy's band: a Brooklyn project he began in 2002, writing, playing and recording nearly everything himself, with a live band built around him. Murphy was a veteran engineer and producer and co-founder of DFA Records, and LCD became the label's flagship. Their records join the physical push of disco and house to the sound of post-punk and art rock: real drums played against drum machines, analog synths, cowbell and claps, and Murphy's half-spoken, half-shouted vocals about scenes, ageing, friendship and endings. Four studio albums (2005-2017), a celebrated farewell at Madison Square Garden in 2011, a return in 2015, and a run of New York residencies since 2021.

**Genre positioning:** Dance-punk, electronic rock, art rock, post-punk revival. DFA's sound is "broadly bounded by synthpop, disco, and punk" (Bandcamp Daily). Alexis Petridis (The Guardian) called them "one of the most acclaimed and influential American bands"; Pitchfork in 2021 called them "one of the most important acts of the preceding 25 years". Murphy calls LCD "a band about bands".

**Commercial scale:** *This Is Happening* (2010) was their first US top ten (No. 10); *American Dream* (2017) went to No. 1 in the US and Canada. Five Grammy nominations and one win: Best Dance Recording for "Tonite" (2018). Every studio album scored 84-86 on Metacritic.

**Key quote:** "I always liked to find something that kind of did one thing for a really long time and did it very well." — James Murphy, 2010

### James Murphy and DFA

- **Background**: Born 1970, Princeton Junction, New Jersey. Played in Falling Man, Pony and Speedking in the late 1980s and 1990s; worked as an engineer (Six Finger Satellite; David Holmes's *Bow Down to the Exit Sign*, 2000)
- **DFA**: Murphy and Tim Goldsworthy first worked as a production duo, "the DFA" ("Death from Above", from *Apocalypse Now*); the label was founded in 2001 with Jonathan Galkin. Its studio and offices were at 225 West 13th Street, New York, home to DFA since 1999. Gavin (now Gavilán Rayna) Russom repaired and modified its synths
- **The studio method**: Murphy has re-recorded bandmates' parts himself ("I happen to be better at being me"), though the live band and several members co-wrote key songs ("All My Friends": Mahoney, Murphy, Pope; "Tonite": Murphy and Doyle)

| Era | Releases | Sound |
|-----|----------|-------|

### LCD Soundsystem (2005)

- **Release date**: 24 January 2005
- **Label**: DFA / Capitol (US), EMI elsewhere
- **Producer**: The DFA (Murphy and Goldsworthy)
- **Recording**: 2002-2004, Long View Farm (North Brookfield, Massachusetts) and Plantain Recording House (DFA's studio, New York)
- **Format**: Two discs; disc 2 collects the earlier singles and B-sides

- Punk guitar and bass over dance grooves

### Sound of Silver (2007)

- **Release date**: 12 March 2007 (UK), 20 March (US)
- **Label**: DFA / Capitol / EMI

## Musical Analysis

### Drums: Machines and a Real Kit

The core of the sound is a drum machine or synth take with real drums played to it: "doing a drum-machine-and-synth take and then going and playing drums live to it" (Murphy, 2007). He records on a 1957 Gretsch jazz kit with the bottom heads removed, and programs parts "without a grid so that they feel more liquid". The TR-808 and TR-606 are favourites, and the kick of a Wurlitzer Sideman is a sample he uses "all the time". Cowbell, claps and hand percussion are signatures.

### Synths

Vintage analog synths and sequencers, used for drones, stabs and long ostinatos: Yamaha CS-60, Moog Taurus driven by a Korg SQ-10 sequencer, EMS Synthi A ("a magical beast"), Roland Jupiter-4 ("Nothing sounds as soft and lush"), SH-101, MS-20, ARP Odyssey, Oberheim SEM. Sounds made on vintage instruments in the studio are copied to smaller synths for the road; live, they tour a real piano: "no samples are allowed".

### Space and Repetition

"I try to not put too much stuff in, and I try also to leave gaps." Songs repeat one idea for a long time and change when the repetition is exhausted: "when I'm bored playing something, it usually lines up with when it's time to change". Many tracks run seven to nine minutes, with slow builds and big releases ("Dance Yrself Clean", "All My Friends").

### Vocals

"Lyrically and vocally, I was never all that interested in melody or great voices." Murphy's delivery moves between talk-singing, a deadpan list ("Losing My Edge"), shouting, and occasional falsetto. Nancy Whang and the band sing backing parts.

### Lyrics

- **Scenes and cool**: "Losing My Edge" lists records to mock the need to be first to them
- **Ageing, friendship and loss**: "All My Friends", "Someone Great", *American Dream*
- **New York**: Love and frustration ("New York, I Love You but You're Bringing Me Down")
- **Self-mockery and sincerity together**: "a band about bands"

### Influences Murphy Names

The Fall ("I was a big Fall fan"), Can ("Here's this for 30 minutes"), David Bowie ("very obsessed… all my life"), Suicide (for its "sheer singularity of vision"), the B-52's and Yes. Critics add Talking Heads.

### Genre Classification

- **Dance-punk**: The primary label, with the DFA scene
- **Electronic rock / dance-rock**: *Sound of Silver*, *This Is Happening*
- **Art rock / post-punk / new wave**: *American Dream*
- **Disco and house**: The grooves underneath

## Reception and Influence

| Album | Metacritic | Selected verdicts |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| *LCD Soundsystem* | 86 | "A sweaty and locked-in live band even when it's just the one guy" (Stereogum) |
| *Sound of Silver* | 86 | "Dance-rock for grown-ups: extraordinary" (Guardian); Pitchfork No. 2 of 2007 |
| *This Is Happening* | 84 | "Most earnest, lovelorn songs" (Rolling Stone) |
| *American Dream* | 86 | "An extraordinary album obsessed with endings" (Pitchfork); US No. 1 |

Slate called them "the north star of hipsterdom", and The Atlantic treated the 2011 farewell as the end of indie rock's cultural moment. Murphy co-produced Arcade Fire's *Reflektor*. Galkin says DFA set out to bridge New York's indie and dance scenes, which had "actively grown stale".
