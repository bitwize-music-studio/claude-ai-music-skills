# Avicii — Deep Dive

## Overview

Avicii was the Swedish DJ, producer and songwriter Tim Bergling (1989-2018). Self-taught on FL Studio as a teenager in Stockholm, he became one of the defining artists of the 2010s EDM boom with "Levels" (2011), a progressive house track built on an Etta James vocal, and then pushed dance music towards folk, bluegrass and country with "Wake Me Up" and the album *True* (2013). His records are built on melody first: big, simple, often minor-key hooks played on piano or a bright synth lead, over four-on-the-floor beats at about 124-128 BPM, sung by guest vocalists. He retired from touring in 2016 and died in 2018, aged 28; the posthumous album *TIM* (2019) was finished by his collaborators.

**Genre positioning:** Progressive house, EDM, house; from 2013 also folktronica and country-pop fusion. Mixmag (2018) credits "Levels" with breaking barriers for electronic music to cross over into pop and radio; The Observer wrote that "Wake Me Up" "tapped into the market potential of mixing EDM and country".

**Commercial scale:** "Wake Me Up" reached No. 1 in 22 countries (UK 1, US 4) and is certified 11× Platinum in the US. "Levels" (Sweden 1, UK 4) passed a billion Spotify streams; "Hey Brother" (UK 2, US 16), "I Could Be the One" (UK 1), "Waiting for Love" and "The Nights" were also major hits. *True* reached No. 5 in the US and No. 2 in the UK. Two Grammy nominations ("Sunshine" with David Guetta, "Levels").

**Key quote:** "I always start with the melody, so I'll sit by the piano and play the chords with the melody… When I have a melody, I'll build everything around that." — Avicii, 2011

**The name:** A respelling of Avīci, the lowest level of hell in Buddhism; his real name was already taken on Myspace.

## Background

- **Born**: Tim Bergling, 8 September 1989, Stockholm
- **Start**: At 16 he taught himself production on FL Studio ("a friend of mine showed me this program where you make your own music and I got stuck instantly") and posted remixes on producer forums, including Laidback Luke's, as "Timberman"
- **First deals**: Signed to the Dejfitts Plays label in May 2007; won a BBC Radio 1 competition in 2008 and signed a management deal with Arash "Ash" Pournouri
- **Aliases**: Tim Berg ("Bromance", "Alcoholic"), Tom Hangs ("Blessed")
- **Labels**: PRMD and Island (*True*, *Stories*); from late 2016 Universal Music Sweden and Geffen (*Avīci (01)*, *TIM*)
- **Influences he named**: Eric Prydz, Swedish House Mafia, Daft Punk
- **Health and retirement**: Hospitalised in 2012 with acute pancreatitis; years of health problems followed. In March 2016 he announced the end of touring: "I have too little left for the life of a real person behind the artist." His last show was at Ushuaïa, Ibiza, on 28 August 2016
- **Death**: He died by suicide on 20 April 2018 in Muscat, Oman, aged 28. His family: "He could not go on any longer. He wanted to find peace." His parents founded the Tim Bergling Foundation (2019), which works on young people's mental health and suicide prevention; Stockholm's Ericsson Globe became the Avicii Arena in 2021

## Releases

### Key Singles Before the Albums

- **"Seek Bromance"** (2010) — Vocal version of "Bromance", re-recorded from an existing vocal; UK 13
- **"Levels"** (28 October 2011) — C♯ minor, ~126 BPM, 5:38 original mix. Built on Etta James's "Something's Got a Hold on Me" (1962), which he found through Pretty Lights' "Finally Moving"; he chose it for its "timeless" and "raw" quality. AllMusic: "simple and effective EDM monster". Sweden 1, UK 4, US 60; Grammy-nominated
- **"Silhouettes"** (2012) — F minor, ~128 BPM; written with Salem Al Fakir, who sings
- **"I Could Be the One"** (with Nicky Romero, 2012) — ~128 BPM; vocals by Noonie Bao; UK No. 1. Digital Spy: "a battle between Balearic-style EDM and squiggly, chart-friendly breakdowns"

### True (2013)

- **Release date**: 13 September 2013 (US 17 September)
- **Label**: PRMD / Island
- **Producers**: Avicii, co-produced by Ash Pournouri; Nile Rodgers co-produced "Lay Me Down"
- **Length**: 46:56
- **Chart**: Sweden 1, UK 2, US 5, US Dance Albums 1; Platinum in the US and UK
- **Reception**: Metacritic 69; Spin: "inhabits his newfound country-pop shtick so naturally"; Billboard's No. 7 dance album of the decade

The folk turn. Avicii: "bluegrass in particular has always captured my attention… The sound of the acoustics is just so pure, especially the guitar", and "to put a 4/4 beat under it and play around and add synths, it really wasn't hard." Before release he played the songs at Ultra with a live band, banjo and kazoo: "We wanted to make a statement."

**Sonic Palette:**
- Acoustic guitar, banjo and folk melodies over four-on-the-floor house beats
- Soul, bluegrass and Americana singers (Aloe Blacc, Dan Tyminski, Audra Mae)
- Piano chords and bright synth leads for the drops
- A Nile Rodgers co-production ("Lay Me Down") and a long progressive track ("Dear Boy")

**Key Tracks:**

- **"Addicted to You"** (C♯ minor, ~128 BPM, 2:28) — Audra Mae's torch-song vocal; short
- **"Dear Boy"** (7:59) — Long progressive build

## Musical Analysis

### Melody First

Avicii described himself as a melody writer, not an engineer: "I'm not the technical producer, I've learned how to use a compressor", and "It's not about your finger skills on an instrument." He sketched melodies and chords first, at the piano or in FL Studio's piano roll, and did the sound design afterwards. The result is hooks that are simple enough to sing or whistle, repeated and built into the drop.

### Tools

"All I've ever needed is my laptop or a computer running FL Studio, Sylenth and some other VSTs" (Future Music, 2011). His 2011 studio had KRK and Genelec monitors and an M-Audio controller keyboard. The "Levels" sound is widely recreated as detuned saw leads doubled with piano stabs and sidechain pumping, though that is a tutorial reconstruction, not his patch.

### Progressive House Structure

Long intros and breakdowns that strip back to piano chords or a vocal, a build with risers and snare rolls, and a drop where the main melody arrives on a bright synth lead over a four-on-the-floor kick with sidechained pads. Radio edits run about three and a half minutes; original mixes five to seven.

### The Folk and Country Fusion

From 2013 he put acoustic songs on top of the same structure: strummed acoustic guitar, banjo, bluegrass and soul vocals for the verses and choruses, then the synth melody at the drop. "Folk is really stripped down… to put a 4/4 beat under it and play around and add synths, it really wasn't hard."

### Vocals

He did not sing; he chose and directed guest vocalists, usually soulful or rootsy voices (Etta James sample, Aloe Blacc, Dan Tyminski, Audra Mae, Nicholas Furlong), and wanted them to match the melody "exactly how he heard it in his head". Lyrics are often about youth, hope and living fully.

### Genre Classification

- **Progressive house / EDM**: The early singles and festival sound
- **Folktronica / country-pop EDM**: *True* and after
- **Dance-pop**: *Stories*, *Avīci (01)*, *TIM*
