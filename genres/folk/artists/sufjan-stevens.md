# Sufjan Stevens — Deep Dive

## Overview

Sufjan Stevens is an American singer, songwriter and multi-instrumentalist from Michigan whose records swing between two extremes: maximal, orchestral folk-pop full of banjo, horns, woodwinds, strings, glockenspiel and choirs of friends (*Michigan*, *Illinois*), and hushed, intimate home recordings of voice, fingerpicked guitar, banjo and piano (*Seven Swans*, *Carrie & Lowell*), with excursions into electronic music (*The Age of Adz*, *The Ascension*). He records much of his work himself at home, playing most of the instruments, and writes about faith, American places and history, love, and grief, above all on *Carrie & Lowell* (2015), about the death of his mother. His label is Asthmatic Kitty, founded with his stepfather, Lowell Brams.

**Genre positioning:** Indie folk, chamber/baroque pop, singer-songwriter, electronic. Stereogum places him, with the Decemberists and Arcade Fire, at the head of a "hyper-literate wave of indie bands". He is influenced by minimalism: asked for his top five albums, he named only Steve Reich's *Music for 18 Musicians*.

**Commercial scale:** *Illinois* (2005) and *Carrie & Lowell* (2015) are certified Gold in the US and UK; *The Age of Adz* reached No. 7 on the Billboard 200, his highest. "Mystery of Love" (*Call Me by Your Name*) was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song (2018) and a Grammy. *Illinoise*, a dance-theatre show built on *Illinois*, ran on Broadway in 2024 and won a Tony.

**Key quote:** "I was recording songs as a means of grieving, making sense of it… It was a year of real darkness." — Sufjan Stevens on *Carrie & Lowell*, 2015

## Background

- **Born**: 1 July 1975, Detroit; moved at nine to Alanson in northern Michigan. Raised by his father and stepmother; his mother, Carrie, lived in Oregon with her second husband, Lowell Brams, and the children spent three summers with them in Eugene
- **Training**: Studied oboe and English horn at school (including Interlochen Arts Academy); learned guitar at Hope College, Michigan; MFA in creative writing from The New School
- **Early bands**: The folk-rock band Marzuki (Holland, Michigan); played in Danielson Famile
- **Asthmatic Kitty**: Founded in 1999 by Holland, Michigan musicians led by Stevens and Lowell Brams, named after Lowell's asthmatic cat
- **Instruments**: On *Michigan* alone he plays oboe, English horn, piano, organ, electric piano, banjo, guitars, bass, vibraphone, xylophone, glockenspiel, recorders, flute, whistles, drums and percussion
- **The Fifty States project**: *Michigan* came with a promise of an album for every US state; only *Illinois* followed. In 2009 he called it "a promotional gimmick" and "such a joke"
- **Later life**: His mother died in 2012; his father in 2020; his partner, Evans Richardson, in 2023, to whom he dedicated *Javelin*. In 2023 he was hospitalised with Guillain–Barré syndrome ("I woke up one morning and couldn't walk"); it took about six months to walk again

## Sonic Evolution Summary

| Era | Releases | Sound |
|-----|----------|-------|
| 2000-2001 | *A Sun Came*, *Enjoy Your Rabbit* | Home four-track folk with many instruments; glitch electronics |
| 2003-2006 | *Michigan*, *Seven Swans*, *Illinois* | Orchestral indie folk: banjo, woodwinds, brass, strings, vibraphone, glockenspiel, choirs; odd meters and minimalist repetition; *Seven Swans* the quiet, banjo-led exception |
| 2010 | *The Age of Adz* | "Zip-zap keyboards and aggressive drum machines" with orchestra and choir; vocoded vocals |
| 2015 | *Carrie & Lowell* | Spare: voice, fingerpicked guitar, banjo, occasional piano and keyboard, found sound. "The horns are gone. The synthesizers are gone" (KEXP) |
| 2020 | *The Ascension* | Synth-pop and electronic: three Prophet synths and a Tempest drum machine. "I got rid of all the idioms of narrative folk songwriting" |
| 2023 | *Javelin* | "Sustained piano chords, fingerpicked acoustic guitars, and whispered singing" that "explode into electro-organic widescreen pageantry" (Stereogum) |

**Tempo range:** Spotify-derived readings run from about 75 to 170 BPM, with many ballads read at double time; several songs are in 3/4 or 5/4.

## Key Albums

### Michigan (2003)

- **Release date**: 1 July 2003
- **Label**: Sounds Familyre / Asthmatic Kitty
- **Producer**: Stevens; recorded in Michigan, Massachusetts, New Jersey and a Brooklyn church on two SM57s and an AKG C1000 into a Roland digital recorder
- **Length**: 65:58
- **Reception**: Pitchfork 8.5: "a beautiful, sprawling homage"

**Key Tracks:** "Flint (For the Unemployed and Underpaid)" (3:43) · "Detroit, Lift Up Your Weary Head!" (8:20) · "Romulus" (4:41) · "Vito's Ordination Song" (7:06)

### Seven Swans (2004)

- **Release date**: 16 March 2004
- **Label**: Sounds Familyre
- **Producer**: Daniel C. Smith
- **Reception**: Metacritic 87; The Guardian: "a record of remarkable delicacy"; Spin: "like Elliott Smith after ten years of Sunday school"

"Lush acoustic compositions" with banjo, on Christian themes (Abraham, the Transfiguration).

**Key Tracks:** "To Be Alone with You" (C minor, ~142 BPM, 2:44) · "Seven Swans" (6:33) · "The Transfiguration" (5:18) · "Sister" (6:00)

### Illinois (2005)

- **Release date**: 4 July 2005 (EU), 5 July (US)
- **Label**: Asthmatic Kitty (US), Rough Trade (EU)
- **Producer**: Stevens; recorded late 2004 – early 2005 in Queens, his Brooklyn apartment, a church and New Jersey
- **Players**: The Illinoisemaker Choir, a string quartet (including Rob Moose), Craig Montoro (trumpet), James McAlister (drums)
- **Length**: 73:59 (22 tracks)
- **Chart**: Billboard 200 No. 121, Heatseekers No. 1; RIAA and BPI Gold
- **Reception**: Metacritic 90; Pitchfork 9.2 ("strange and lush, as excessive and challenging as its giant, gushing song titles"); Paste's No. 1 album of the 2000s

He usually laid down a base of banjo, acoustic guitar or piano and added parts one at a time; "banjo-plucking… is a core sound on the record". He read Sandburg and Bellow and studied immigration records for the lyrics. "Come On! Feel the Illinoise!" opens in 5/4; "Out of Egypt…" shows the Reich influence most clearly.

**Sonic Palette:**
- Banjo, acoustic guitar, piano and Wurlitzer
- Trumpet, woodwinds, strings, vibraphone, glockenspiel
- Choirs of friends singing "childlike chorales" (Stereogum)
- Odd meters, repeating minimalist figures, short instrumental interludes with very long titles

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Chicago"** (G major, ~125 BPM, 6:04) — Credits list trumpet, violins and cello, a five-voice choir, vibraphone and Wurlitzer; booming choruses against near-quiet verses
- **"Casimir Pulaski Day"** (A♭ major, ~134 BPM, 5:53) — A quiet song about a friend's death from cancer, faith and doubt
- **"John Wayne Gacy, Jr."** (C, ~81 BPM, 3:19) — A hushed song about the serial killer, turning to self-examination
- **"The Predatory Wasp of the Palisades Is Out to Get Us!"** (E major, 3/4, 5:23)
- **"Come On! Feel the Illinoise!"** (6:45) — 5/4 into 4/4

### The Age of Adz (2010)

- **Release date**: 12 October 2010
- **Label**: Asthmatic Kitty
- **Producer**: Stevens
- **Length**: 74:49
- **Chart**: Billboard 200 No. 7
- **Reception**: Metacritic 80; Pitchfork 8.4: "Impossible Soul" has "more engaging ideas than most artists could muster in a career"

Cover art by the outsider artist Royal Robertson. Electronic beats and keyboards over orchestral and choral arrangements; the 25-minute "Impossible Soul" has "a broken-vocoder plea" at its centre (Stereogum).

**Key Tracks:** "Futile Devices" (E major, ~140 BPM, 2:11; quiet acoustic opener) · "Impossible Soul" (B minor, ~120 BPM, 25:35) · "Too Much" (6:44) · "I Want to Be Well" (6:27)

### Carrie & Lowell (2015)

- **Release date**: 31 March 2015
- **Label**: Asthmatic Kitty
- **Producers**: Stevens and Thomas Bartlett (Doveman)
- **Recording**: 2014, in New York, Portland, Oklahoma and Wisconsin studios and his office, plus an iPhone recording in a hotel room
- **Length**: 43:35
- **Chart**: Billboard 200 No. 10, UK No. 6; Gold in the US and UK
- **Reception**: Metacritic 90; Pitchfork 9.3 ("his best"; 10/10 for the 2025 reissue); The Guardian's No. 2 album of 2015 and No. 37 of the century

About the death of his mother, Carrie, and his summers with her and Lowell in Oregon. "Grieving is eternal and you never really get over it" (2025).

**Sonic Palette:**
- "Sparse with guitar and banjo plucks, an occasional piano chord" (Relix)

- "Processed found sound" and drones between songs
- Thomas Bartlett's piano ("Should Have Known Better"); Laura Veirs's backing vocals

**Key Tracks:**

- **"Should Have Known Better"** (G major, ~91 BPM, 5:07) — Fingerpicked guitar with Thomas Bartlett's piano and Laura Veirs's backing vocals
- **"Fourth of July"** (F♯ minor, ~120 BPM, 4:39) — "A tender song about his mother's death": piano, a drone and tape hiss
- **"Carrie & Lowell"** (A minor, ~99 BPM, 3:14)
- **"No Shade in the Shadow of the Cross"** (F♯ major, ~83 BPM, 2:40)

**Tracklist:** Death with Dignity 3:59 · Should Have Known Better 5:07 · All of Me Wants All of You 3:41 · Drawn to the Blood 3:18 · Eugene 2:26 · Fourth of July 4:39 · The Only Thing 4:44 · Carrie & Lowell 3:14 · John My Beloved 5:04 · No Shade in the Shadow of the Cross 2:40 · Blue Bucket of Gold 4:43

## Musical Analysis

### Home Recording, One Part at a Time

From the start he recorded himself: a four-track for *A Sun Came*, then "a cheap digital 8-track" dumped into Pro Tools and mixed on headphones (Tape Op). "I still have the recorder. I've used it on almost every record." He builds songs from a base of banjo, guitar or piano and layers instruments one by one, then adds friends' choirs and guest players. He is modest about it: "I'm really slow and clumsy."

### Instrumentation

- **Folk core**: banjo, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, piano, Wurlitzer
- **Orchestral colour**: oboe and English horn (his own instruments), flute, recorders, trumpet and brass, strings, vibraphone, xylophone, glockenspiel
- **Voices**: Choirs of friends and backing singers; "childlike chorales"
- **Electronics**: Glitch (*Enjoy Your Rabbit*), drum machines and vocoder (*Adz*), Prophet synths and the Tempest (*The Ascension*), Melodyne on vocals

### Voice

A soft "boyish tenor" (Relix), often "hushed" or whispered, with breathy layered harmonies. He says "I'm not a great singer."

### Composition: Meter and Minimalism

Odd meters (5/4 in "Come On! Feel the Illinoise!", 3/4 in "The Predatory Wasp…", 5/4 in "Shit Talk") and repeating, interlocking figures from minimalism: Steve Reich above all, plus Terry Riley, Philip Glass, Stravinsky, Rachmaninoff and Grieg.

### Themes

- **Faith**: Christian imagery and doubt (*Seven Swans*, "Casimir Pulaski Day"). "God is my only hope… I still rely on God in everything I do"
- **American places and history**: *Michigan*, *Illinois* (UFO sightings, Chicago, serial killers, Superman, the Pullman strike)
- **Grief**: His mother (*Carrie & Lowell*), his father (*Convocations*), his partner (*Javelin*)
- **Love and longing**: *Javelin*, "Mystery of Love"
- **Childhood memory**: Summers in Oregon, specific places and objects

### Genre Classification

- **Indie folk / singer-songwriter**: The primary label; *Seven Swans*, *Carrie & Lowell*
- **Baroque / chamber pop**: *Michigan*, *Illinois*
- **Electronic / art pop**: *Enjoy Your Rabbit*, *The Age of Adz*, *The Ascension*
- **Ambient / new age**: *Convocations*, *Aporia*
