# Flogging Molly — Deep Dive

## Overview

Flogging Molly is a Los Angeles Celtic punk band led by Dublin-born singer Dave King, a former hard-rock frontman (Fastway) who left the metal world to play Irish music with punk drive. The band grew out of a weekly Monday-night residency at Molly Malone's, an Irish pub in Los Angeles, in the mid-1990s, and released its first record, a live album recorded there, in 1997. Seven studio albums followed (2000-2022), with fiddle, tin whistle, uilleann pipes, accordion, mandolin and banjo over electric guitar, bass and drums, and lyrics about Ireland and its history, emigration, drink, poverty, the Church and King's late father. *Float* (2008) reached No. 4 on the Billboard 200.

**Genre positioning:** Celtic punk, Irish folk-punk, folk rock. Punknews (2002) called them "truly a Celtic-punk band, not simply a punk band dabbling with Celtic influences". They are the West Coast counterpart to Boston's Dropkick Murphys, with whom they have co-headlined since 2018; bassist Nathen Maxwell calls the Murphys "our east-coast counterparts".

**Commercial scale:** *Drunken Lullabies* is certified Gold in the US; *Within a Mile of Home* reached No. 20, *Float* No. 4 and *Speed of Darkness* No. 9 on the Billboard 200. The *Whiskey on a Sunday* film is certified Platinum as a long-form video.

**Key quote:** "Irish music has always been punk to me… It always had that attitude." — Dave King, 2024

**The name:** King: "We used to play there every Monday night and we felt like we were flogging it to death, so we called the band Flogging Molly."

| **John Donovan** | Guitar | *Swagger* |

Early players also included Jeff Peters (bass), Paul Crowder (drums) and Toby McCallum (mandolin).

**Dave King's background:** Born in Dublin and raised in a two-room flat in Beggars Bush; his father died when he was ten. He sang in Fastway, the band formed by Motörhead's "Fast" Eddie Clarke and UFO's Pete Way (1982-1986), then fronted Katmandu (1991, with future Flogging Molly drummer Mike Alonso). Epic Records objected to traditional Irish instruments, and he negotiated his way out of the deal. He met fiddler Bridget Regan, now his wife, at Molly Malone's.

**Formation date:** Sources differ: Wikipedia has the members meeting in 1993 and forming the band in 1994; other sources date it from 1997, the year of the first release.

## Sonic Evolution Summary

| Era | Releases | Sound |
|-----|----------|-------|
| 1997-2002 | *Alive Behind the Green Door*, *Swagger*, *Drunken Lullabies* | Raw, live-recorded folk-punk with Albini: "thrash-jig anthems and moshable drinking songs" |
| 2004-2008 | *Within a Mile of Home*, *Float* | "More mature, more polished": wider arrangements, guest vocals (Lucinda Williams), more ballads; *Float* "more assured" and recorded in Ireland |
| 2011 | *Speed of Darkness* | Darker and more political, about Detroit's economic collapse; "musically… very adventurous"; smoother production |
| 2017-2022 | *Life Is Good*, *Anthem* | *Life Is Good*: Celtic-Tiger aftermath, mariachi trumpets, uilleann pipes; *Anthem*: back to live recording with Albini |

**Tempo range:** Spotify-derived readings run from about 105 BPM for the ballads ("If I Ever Leave This World Alive", "Float") to 155-170 for the fast songs; most anthems sit at 136-160.

## Studio Albums

### Swagger (2000)

- **Release date**: 7 March 2000
- **Label**: SideOneDummy
- **Producer**: Flogging Molly; engineered and mixed by Steve Albini at Electrical Audio, Chicago
- **Length**: 53:40
- **Chart**: Billboard 200 No. 128 (one week, in 2011)

King on Albini: he would "record you, and basically let you do what you wanted to do with the rest of it yourself". AllMusic: "music that's perfect for any barroom brawl." Punknews (2001) found some of "the rock parts feel forced" but no weak songs.

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Salty Dog"** (G major, ~160 BPM, 2:21) — Fast, short opener
- **"The Worst Day Since Yesterday"** (D major, ~136 BPM, 3:38) — Drinking-song singalong
- **"Black Friday Rule"** (B minor, ~142 BPM, 6:57) — Long, building live favourite (nearly twelve minutes on *Whiskey on a Sunday*)
- **"Devil's Dance Floor"** (~145 BPM, 3:59) — Fast reel energy
- **"The Likes of You Again"** (4:33) — A tribute to King's father

### Drunken Lullabies (2002)

- **Release date**: 19 March 2002
- **Label**: SideOneDummy
- **Producers**: Flogging Molly and Ted Hutt; recorded by Albini at Electrical Audio
- **Length**: 45:18
- **Chart**: Billboard 200 peak No. 104 (in 2010); RIAA Gold (2009)

Their best-known album. Phoenix New Times (2002): "signature thrash-jig anthems and moshable drinking songs". Punknews: "truly a Celtic-punk band". It closes with Pete St. John's Dublin song "The Rare Ould Times".

**Key Tracks:**
- **"Drunken Lullabies"** (G major, ~156 BPM, 3:50) — King: "The second the banjo riff comes in, people just lose their minds… It's about the problems that were happening in Ireland when I was growing up"
- **"What's Left of the Flag"** (G major, ~155 BPM, 3:39) — A tribute to King's father; King has also called it a song "about freedom"
- **"If I Ever Leave This World Alive"** (D major, ~105 BPM, 3:21) — The ballad; first released live in 1997, later on the *P.S. I Love You* soundtrack
- **"Rebels of the Sacred Heart"** (5:11) — With "What's Left of the Flag", noted for "mandolin, a feisty fiddle and lilting tin whistle" (Phoenix New Times)

**Tracklist:** Drunken Lullabies 3:50 · What's Left of the Flag 3:39 · May the Living Be Dead (In Our Wake) 3:50 · If I Ever Leave This World Alive 3:21 · The Kilburn High Road 3:43 · Rebels of the Sacred Heart 5:11 · Swagger 2:05 · Cruel Mistress 2:57 · Death Valley Queen 4:18 · Another Bag of Bricks 3:45 · The Rare Ould Times (Pete St. John) 4:06 · The Son Never Shines (On Closed Doors) 4:25

## Musical Analysis

### Instrumentation

- **Fiddle, tin whistle, uilleann pipes** (Bridget Regan): the lead melodic voices. King: "When I heard Bridget playing the fiddle it brought out a lot of feelings in me."
- **Accordion and concertina** (Matt Hensley)
- **Mandolin, mandola, banjo, bouzouki** (Bob Schmidt; later Spencer Swain); the banjo riff is the hook of "Drunken Lullabies"
- **Bodhrán, spoons, acoustic guitar** (Dave King)
- **Electric guitar, bass, drums**: the punk-rock engine underneath
- **Guests**: cello (Joe Kwon), mariachi trumpets ("Welcome to Adamstown"), uilleann pipes (Neillidh Mulligan)

### Dave King's Voice

A Dublin brogue, often shouted: "perfect snarling brogue" (PopMatters), "gruffly delivered brogue" (Phoenix New Times). He uses his "shrill shriek" for climaxes and sings the ballads more softly.

### Songwriting and Themes

- **Ireland and its history**: Cromwell's transportations ("Tobacco Island"), the Easter Rising ("A Song of Liberty"), 1798 ("The Croppy Boy '98"), the Celtic Tiger crash ("Welcome to Adamstown")
- **King's father**: "A lot of the lyrics, especially in the early days, were about my dad because I never really grieved over him" ("What's Left of the Flag", "The Likes of You Again")
- **Emigration and exile**: "These Exiled Years", "(No More) Paddy's Lament", "Man with No Country"
- **Drink, poverty, the Church, working-class politics**: "Drunken Lullabies", Detroit on *Speed of Darkness*
- King: "We don't try and rehash anything, taking old Irish songs and bashing them to death." Their songs are originals, with occasional covers ("The Rare Ould Times")

### Recording

The best-known records were made live in the room with Steve Albini (*Swagger*, *Drunken Lullabies*, *Anthem*), keeping the band's live attack. The mid-period albums with Ted Hutt and Ryan Hewitt are more layered and polished; critics split on whether that helped (*Float*) or hurt (*Speed of Darkness*).

### Influences

King: "When you hear bands like the Dubliners or Horslips doing their thing in the '70s, and then the Pogues — they took it to a new generation." He also names the Clash, Bob Marley and Johnny Cash, and says folk music "should be soul-searching and rabble-rousing, you know, like the Dubliners".

### Genre Classification

- **Celtic punk**: The primary label
- **Irish folk / folk rock**: Ballads, acoustic records, traditional instrumentation
- **Punk rock**: Warped Tour roots, speed and volume
