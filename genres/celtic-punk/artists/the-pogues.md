# The Pogues — Deep Dive

## Overview

The Pogues were a London band that played Irish traditional music with the speed, volume and attitude of punk. Shane MacGowan, Spider Stacy and Jem Finer formed the band in King's Cross in 1982, first as Pogue Mahone, and over seven studio albums (1984-1996) they went from ramshackle reels and rebel songs to a band that could take in Spanish fiesta brass, Middle Eastern modes, jazz and a string-laden Christmas ballad. MacGowan was sacked in 1991; the band carried on without him until 1996, re-formed with him from 2001 to 2014, and re-formed again without him in 2024.

**Genre positioning:** Celtic punk, Irish folk-punk, folk rock. Celtic punk was "popularised in the 1980s by the Pogues", and most consider them "the prototypical Celtic punk band" (Wikipedia). They did not invent the fusion outright: the Skids' *Joy* (1981) is named as possibly the first UK punk record with a strong folk element. What the Pogues set was the template: traditional tunes and new songs in the same voice, tin whistle, banjo and accordion over punk drums, and a singer whose lyrics drew on emigration, London Irish life, drink, death and Catholicism.

**Commercial scale:** Three UK Top 5 albums (*If I Should Fall from Grace with God*, No. 3; *Peace and Love*, No. 5) and two Top 10 singles ("The Irish Rover" with The Dubliners, No. 8; "Fairytale of New York", No. 2). "Fairytale of New York" returns to the UK chart every Christmas and has made the Top 20 more than twenty times; it has never reached No. 1 in the UK, but went to No. 1 in Ireland after MacGowan's death in 2023.

**Key quote:** "We're not an acoustic band, we're a loud band with a big sound." — Shane MacGowan, 1988

**The name:** "Pogue Mahone" anglicises the Irish *póg mo thóin* ("kiss my arse"); Stacy reportedly took it from Joyce's *Ulysses*. After a BBC Radio Scotland producer complained, DJ David Jensen started calling them "the Pogues" on air, and the band adopted it.

## Sonic Evolution Summary

| Era | Releases | Sound |
|-----|----------|-------|
| 1984 | *Red Roses for Me* | Fast, raw folk-punk: tin whistle, banjo, accordion, bass and drums; MacGowan originals beside traditional songs. "An unholy amphetamine gallop" (Q) |
| 1985-1986 | *Rum Sodomy & the Lash*, *Poguetry in Motion* | Fuller and more varied under Elvis Costello: guitar, guest uilleann pipes, fiddle and French horn; funeral ballads beside spaghetti-western instrumentals; live and natural |
| 1987-1988 | "Fairytale of New York", *If I Should Fall from Grace with God* | Widest palette: cittern and concertina, saxophone, brass, harp and strings; Turkish and Spanish styles; polished Lillywhite production |
| 1989-1990 | *Peace and Love*, *Hell's Ditch* | Jazzier, then rock and folk-rock; traditional material largely dropped; Asian themes on *Hell's Ditch* |
| 1993-1996 | *Waiting for Herb*, *Pogue Mahone* | Without MacGowan; Spider Stacy sings. Back-to-the-roots pub folk, some airy, new-age-tinged whistle |
| 2001-present | Live | The catalogue played live: reunions with MacGowan, then guest singers from 2024 |

**Tempo range:** Spotify-derived readings run from about 78 BPM ("Fairytale of New York") to about 166 counted in three ("A Pair of Brown Eyes"). The fast songs sit at 123-153 BPM; the waltzes ("A Pair of Brown Eyes", "The Sick Bed of Cúchulainn", "The Old Main Drag", "A Rainy Night in Soho") are in 3/4.

### Rum Sodomy & the Lash (1985)

- **Release date**: 5 August 1985
- **Label**: Stiff (UK), MCA (US)
- **Producer**: Elvis Costello
- **Studio**: Elephant Studios
- **Length**: 42:55
- **Chart**: UK 13; BPI Gold
- **Title**: From a quotation attributed to Churchill, suggested by Andrew Ranken

Costello set out to capture the band "in their dilapidated glory"; Philip Chevron, who joined on guitar, said the band needed to be facilitated rather than produced, with a live, natural acoustic sound. Q (1994) called it a "proud, defiant bruise of an album", and NME (1985) noted its range from funereal ballads to spaghetti-western instrumentals. It is in *1001 Albums You Must Hear Before You Die*; Pitchfork placed it No. 67 among the albums of the 1980s.

**Sonic Palette:**
- Adds Chevron's guitar, and guest uilleann pipes, fiddle and French horn
- Slow ballads given room: "A Pair of Brown Eyes", "Dirty Old Town", an eight-minute "And the Band Played Waltzing Matilda"
- Fast reels and stomps beside them ("Sally MacLennane", "The Wild Cats of Kilkenny")
- Live, natural acoustic sound

## "Fairytale of New York"

- **Writers**: Jem Finer wrote the melody and concept, MacGowan the lyrics; work began in 1985
- **Origin**: MacGowan said it came from an Elvis Costello bet that they couldn't write a Christmas hit; manager Frank Murray said the Christmas-song idea was his
- **Recording**: A first version (January 1986) had Cait O'Riordan on the female part. In August 1987 producer Steve Lillywhite had Kirsty MacColl record a guide vocal; MacGowan re-sang alongside her tape, and the two never recorded together
- **Release**: 23 November 1987, Pogue Mahone label, 4:33
- **Sound**: A slow piano-and-voice opening (~78 BPM, D) that opens out into a full band and string arrangement, with a male-female duet that turns from tender to a shouting match and back. MacGowan's vocal is "growlingly tender" (Irish Times)
- **Charts**: UK No. 2 in 1987 (behind the Pet Shop Boys' "Always on My Mind"); it has re-entered every Christmas since 2005, peaking at No. 3 (2005) and No. 4 several times, with 142 weeks in the Top 100. No. 1 in Ireland in December 2023
- **Lyric controversy**: BBC Radio 1 edited two slurs in 2007 (then reversed the decision) and again in 2020; Radio 2 has played a label radio edit since 2022. MacGowan, in 2018: "She is not supposed to be a nice person, or even a wholesome person."

## Musical Analysis

### Instrumentation

The Pogues' sound is built on folk instruments played as a rock band:
- **Tin whistle** (Spider Stacy): the high lead voice on the fast songs, and the "airy whistle" of the later records
- **Accordion** (James Fearnley): melody and drive, and the pulse of the waltzes
- **Banjo** (Jem Finer): rhythmic chop and runs
- **Mandolin, cittern, concertina** (Terry Woods, from 1986), **mandola** (Finer), **uilleann pipes** and **fiddle** (guests)
- **Bass and drums** (O'Riordan or Hunt; Ranken): punk drive under reels and jigs, or a slow 3/4 under ballads
- **Electric and acoustic guitar** (MacGowan, Chevron)
- **From 1987**: saxophone, brass, harp and strings; Spanish fairground brass, Middle Eastern modes, jazz

MacGowan's rule: "We're not an acoustic band, we're a loud band with a big sound." He called The Dubliners "a rock and roll band".

### Shane MacGowan's Voice and Lyrics

- **Voice**: Rough, slurred, often half-shouted on the fast songs, tender on the ballads. Tom Waits called it a "torrid and mighty voice"; Nick Cave counted it among "the most terrifyingly beautiful"
- **Themes**: The emigrant experience, Irish history, the diaspora and London life; drink, death and burial; Catholic and religious imagery. The Irish Times wrote that he "tapped into the diaspora experience like none before"
- **Places**: Real London streets and districts ("Transmetropolitan", "Dark Streets of London", "The Old Main Drag", "A Rainy Night in Soho", "White City"), and New York in "Fairytale"
- **Story songs**: Wakes, deathbeds, emigrant arrivals and soldiers' tales, often from inside a character
- MacGowan, 1985: "A lot of Irish pubs do both rock and Irish bands, so it's surprising that nobody thought of mixing them before." And: "Irish music is guts, balls and feet music" (attributed)

### Songwriting

MacGowan wrote most of the early originals; Finer co-wrote many of the best-known ("Fairytale of New York", "Bottle of Smoke", "Fiesta") and wrote more as time went on. Chevron ("Thousands Are Sailing", "Lorelei"), Woods ("Young Ned of the Hill", "Rainbow Man"), Hunt and Ranken also wrote. Traditional songs and covers (Ewan MacColl, Eric Bogle, Brendan Behan, Bob Dylan) sit beside the originals on most albums, arranged in the same style.

### Fast Songs and Ballads

The records alternate two modes:
- **Fast**: reels, jigs and polkas at about 125-155 BPM, played with punk drive ("Streams of Whiskey", "Sally MacLennane", "Bottle of Smoke", "If I Should Fall from Grace with God")
- **Slow**: waltzes and laments, often in 3/4 ("A Pair of Brown Eyes", "The Old Main Drag", "A Rainy Night in Soho"), and songs that move from one to the other ("The Body of an American", "The Sick Bed of Cúchulainn")

Live, the shows were "closer to punk rock shows than reverent ballad sessions" (Irish Times).

### Production by Era

1. **Stan Brennan (1984)**: raw and fast, a record of the live show
2. **Elvis Costello (1985-1986)**: live and natural; the band "in their dilapidated glory"
3. **Steve Lillywhite (1987-1989)**: polished and full, with strings, brass and a wider range of styles
4. **Joe Strummer (1990)**: rock and folk-rock
5. **Michael Brook, Steve Brown (1993-1996)**: lighter, pub-folk records without MacGowan

### Genre Classification

- **Celtic punk**: The primary label; they popularised it
- **Irish folk / traditional**: Traditional tunes, instruments and song forms throughout
- **Folk rock**: *Hell's Ditch* and the later records
- **World and jazz touches**: Spanish, Middle Eastern, Asian and jazz elements from 1988 to 1990

### The Traditional-Music Response

Some traditional musicians objected at first: Noel Hill's "terrible abortion" remark led to "Planxty Noel Hill". The band were "accepted into the canon" after recording "The Irish Rover" with The Dubliners (Irish Times).

### Influence

- **Celtic punk**: Dropkick Murphys list them among their early influences; Ken Casey (2024): "Without Shane McGowan and The Pogues, there'd be no Dropkick Murphys." MacGowan sang on the Dropkick Murphys' *Sing Loud, Sing Proud!* (2001). Flogging Molly name The Dubliners and the Pogues among their influences
- **Irish folk**: The Irish Times names Lankum, the Mary Wallopers and Lisa O'Neill as following their approach
- **Tributes**: *20th Century Paddy – The Songs of Shane MacGowan* (2026)

See also [Dropkick Murphys — Deep Dive](dropkick-murphys.md).
